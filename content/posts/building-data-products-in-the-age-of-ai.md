As I reflect on what it means to be a data professional today, I see four broad functions that are converging. 

* **D**elivery: modeling, transformation, validation, computation, storage, pipelines. Everything needed to get data fast and accurately from where it's generated to where it is consumed. The traditional function of the Data Engineer. 
* **A**nalysis: the essential bridge from data to action. Though it must be technically enabled, a lead analyst at its core is less a technical role and more one of relationship management - defining analysis parameters serve stakeholder objectives. 
* **T**ooling: Creating effective UX around self-service extraction of meaning from data. It may mean dashboards, it may mean embedding semantic AI-enabled capabilities, or effective processes for collaboration between stakeholders and analysts.
* **A**/B Testing: The most effective way of establishing causal inference. This is the holy grail of continuous discovery - finding the X that drives the Y that is the north-star metric for the business.

First off, the basic truth is that data is deeply embedded in virtually all business functions represented in the C-Suite. Product, Marketing, Finance, Technology, People - all run on data.
